# Offline TTS shortlist for VoxBridge

Research snapshot: 2026-10-15; follow-up checked PyPI vieneu 3.8.3 and the official SDK `main` docs. Scope is a small, official-source shortlist for a Mac CPU-first local audition, with Android compatibility and redistribution terms called out. Local auditions and ASR proxies are recorded below. The user listened to the supplied samples and preferred VieNeu over Piper; this is subjective feedback, not a formal blind listening/MOS score.

## Current baseline and interpretation

The manifest selects Piper `vi_VN-vais1000-medium` and `en_US-lessac-medium`, each as a sherpa-onnx VITS/Piper bundle. The local ONNX files are about 60 MB each; the Vietnamese release archive is pinned at 67,154,040 bytes. The Vietnamese card says one speaker, medium quality, 22.05 kHz, VAIS1000 dataset CC BY 4.0, and fine-tuning from Lessac. The English voice card points to the Lessac/Blizzard dataset terms. These dataset statements do not by themselves clear the trained voice/model artifacts or their redistribution; the repo's package review correctly leaves voice licensing unresolved. See [manifest](../../models/MODEL_MANIFEST.md), [VI card](../../models/text_to_speech/piper/vits-piper-vi_VN-vais1000-medium/MODEL_CARD), [EN card](../../models/text_to_speech/piper/vits-piper-en_US-lessac-medium/MODEL_CARD), and [license review](../../submission/phase-3/package/licenses.md).

Production currently sets `noiseScale=0`, `noiseScaleW=0`, `lengthScale=1`, and speed 1. sherpa documents the common Piper values as 0.667, 0.8, and 1.0; Piper describes the first two as overall speech variability and stochastic phoneme-width/duration variation. Zeroing them can change prosody and naturalness, so it is a plausible listening variable, but official docs do not say it fixes intelligibility. Both settings were compared on the cached voice; local results are recorded below.

The supplied-audio ASR measurement is a separate signal: 10 Vietnamese WAVs produced WER 9/78 (11.54%), CER 17/272 (6.25%), median 428.5 ms, and 3/10 normalized exact. Those are ASR results on supplied WAVs, not a TTS quality score and not evidence that generated speech caused recognition errors.

## Recommendation and bounded audition

Audition **VieNeu-TTS v3 Turbo first** on the Mac, alongside the current `vais1000-medium` at both noise settings that the parent is already testing. It is the clearest match for the complaint and constraints: Vietnamese-first voices, explicit English code-switching, a supported CPU/ONNX path on Apple Silicon, and a declared Apache package license. This recommends continued evaluation, not replacement: the initial M2 run is substantially slower than Piper, and its two-text single-ASR proxy is too small to establish a quality winner. The official RTF figures are author measurements on a 6-core desktop, distinct from the parent-run M2 timings below. Keep current Piper as the Android-compatible baseline while assessing whether a custom VieNeu ONNX pipeline is justified.

For a later Android/weak-CPU branch, Nano is more relevant than MMS because it is Apache-licensed and explicitly targets edge/ARM. Its English and mixed-language quality is weaker, and current materials still require custom ONNX Runtime integration; it is not sherpa-compatible. MMS remains the sherpa VITS conversion option if that integration fit outweighs its CC-BY-NC restriction.

The 1.68 GB Turbo figure above is the full HF snapshot at revision `62ee74b`, not the minimal runtime payload. Parent is investigating Turbo FP32 using local frozen artifacts: model revision `61b85e3d937fbbacb387714180e8182823512523` and codec revision `ceff0d0749bfb3fa2d61149794ec6feef0d1e1ae`. This narrower artifact path is under separate local evaluation and does not establish quality or change the model's sherpa compatibility.

### Preliminary local audition (parent-run; 2026-10-15)

On an M2 Mac with 16 GiB RAM, VieNeu SDK 3.8.3 FP32 ONNX using the frozen artifact subset (520,650,240 bytes) had a 4,773 ms cold load and 1,213.9 MiB peak process memory. Four renders (two Vietnamese texts × Hải Đăng and Trúc Ly) took 1,089–1,262 ms, RTF 0.294–0.316. Existing Piper produced four renders in 163–190 ms, RTF 0.047–0.050, across its current 0/0 and model-card-noise configurations.

Diagnostic PhoBase ASR normalized exact matches on the two texts were: Trúc Ly 2/2, current Piper 0/2, default-noise Piper 1/2, Hải Đăng 0/2. This is a biased single-ASR proxy on only two texts, not a human MOS/listening study or evidence that a model is universally more intelligible. The measurements show this VieNeu path was slower than Piper on this Mac; recognition evidence is too small to declare a quality winner. Four additional Vietnamese probes have now completed. Across all six shared texts, current Piper had 18/71 word edits (25.35%) and 0/6 normalized-exact sentences; Turbo Trúc Ly had 2/71 (2.82%) and 4/6 exact. The remaining recognized differences were “bar” → “ba” and “tôi chưa nghe rõ” → “tôi trở nghe rõ”. These results select Trúc Ly as a candidate for further listening, not a proven production replacement. See [full host evidence](../testing/host-validation.md).

Keep the comparison small and blind: use the same 6–10 representative VoxBridge utterances (North and South Vietnamese, tone/number-heavy text, and one VI/EN mixed sentence), match text and playback loudness, and include the parent’s current-vs-default-noise Piper render as controls. Judge whether listeners transcribe each exactly and whether voice, tone, and code-switching sound acceptable; record generation time, model footprint, and peak memory on the target Mac. Do not interpret the supplied-WAV ASR results as a TTS score. If sherpa-native Android deployment takes priority over permissive licensing, only then audition MMS-VI and decide whether its CC-BY-NC terms are acceptable.

### Local Nano audition on M2

Luna also ran the official Nano FP32 runtime, SDK 3.8.3, preset Trúc Ly, CPU 2 threads, 16 default steps. Pinned model revision `aba295eb96a6fa6003ebe417cc1f2802a7adc1dc`; six required runtime files total **281,756,486 bytes**. Cold load **676 ms**, peak process RSS **420.25 MiB**. Safety text took **1,555 ms** for 5.08 seconds audio; measurement text **1,317 ms** for 4.40 seconds audio (RTF 0.306 / 0.299). No explicit warmup preceded the first render, unlike the Turbo experiment, so this is not a controlled latency ranking. Lower model/RAM footprint is established for these runs; quality and Android integration are not.

The two 16-step Nano WAVs gave PhoBase one word edit out of 36 words (2.78%), one of two sentences exact; the measurement sentence added “mươi” before “li mét”. The bounded 8-step follow-up rendered the same texts in 896 / 737 ms (RTF 0.177 / 0.165). Both configurations lack a separate warmup before their first text and use different synthesis draws/seeds; the speed figures are preliminary and do not establish equal quality. The two 8-step WAVs yielded 0/36 word edits and 2/2 normalized-exact sentences through the same PhoBase proxy. This does not show that eight steps generally sound better than sixteen or that safety acceptance passed. Total Nano render budget was four. Full local details and limitations: [host evidence](../testing/host-validation.md).

### Primary sources checked

Android integration follow-up: the official SEA-G2P 0.10.0 Rust package provides an optional `capi` feature with `sea_g2p_open`, `sea_g2p_phonemize`, normalization and explicit string/handle free functions. `cargo build --release --no-default-features --features capi` removes PyO3. The owner repository supplies Apache-2.0 licensing. This identifies a native phonemizer route without embedding Python, but **the Android library and Nano runtime adapter have not been built/integrated**; the selected host bridge is not that port. Primary source: [Cargo features](https://github.com/pnnbao97/sea-g2p/blob/main/Cargo.toml), [C ABI](https://github.com/pnnbao97/sea-g2p/blob/main/src/capi.rs), [license](https://github.com/pnnbao97/sea-g2p/blob/main/LICENSE).
