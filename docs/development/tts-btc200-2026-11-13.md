Only case, whitespace and one terminal period/exclamation are normalized. Questions, extra clauses, numbers, negation and internal punctuation cannot be discarded to obtain a match. ## Same-output paired experiment

Used all 395 permitted translation outputs from prior full-audio BTC200 × two MT models (197 EnViT5, 198 VinAI). Other five outputs are cached no-speech/ask-repeat prompts. No fresh ASR/MT and no quality-reference inputs used. Plans were generated with actual Kotlin `SpeechChunker`, `SpokenTextNormalizer` and cache lookup via `TtsPlanCli.kt`, avoiding a separate Python implementation. Each output rendered twice, full text and sentence chunks, alternating paired order. Both voices warmed first; CPU two threads. Original whole-utterance WAVs preserved.

For 59 multi-chunk outputs, first-ready median falls about 214 ms (63%); total synthesis median is 328 ms, p95 686 ms. 336 single-chunk outputs receive no sentence-streaming benefit. None of the 395 translations exactly matches the command bank: zero BTC200 cache speedup, no cache hit rate claim. Voice initialization separately took 1023 ms EN / 1043 ms VI on this busy VPS; warm figures do not include loading/switching voices.

These are VPS **synthesis-to-first-PCM** times, not end-of-speech to acoustic first audio. The earlier 200-case audio pipeline results are not overwritten or re-labelled. No new end-to-end/phone <2 s claim. Sentence splitting retains text but may change prosody; human listening and target-device playback/cancel/voice-switch latency remain necessary. Existing chunker abbreviation behavior is unchanged.
